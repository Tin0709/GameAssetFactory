# Technical strategies: documented tools, unknown pack internals, original adaptations

2026-10-14. Research only. No animation, Showcase, rig, Godot, asset or production changes. This expands the technical questions in [the preceding locomotion study](../../ACTIONS_AND_STUFF_LOCOMOTION_STUDY.md); it does not repeat its footage analysis or claim to recover Oreville's curves.

**D** = directly documented capability or product behavior; **O-prior** = observation recorded in the preceding study, not a new independent inspection here; **M-local** = inspected local source/data; **H** = interpretation; **P** = our proposed implementation/test; **U** = unestablished. Engine documentation proves what that engine permits. It does not identify the implementation of Actions & Stuff. In particular, Molang availability is not evidence that Oreville used procedural sine motion, a phase accumulator, IK, a spring, or a particular controller hierarchy.

## What “sharp but continuous” means mathematically

**P — design specification:** continuity has levels. Equal endpoint position is C0; matching velocity is C1; matching acceleration is C2. A constant segment followed by motion can keep position continuous while introducing an acceleration or velocity defect. Conversely, a short, high-acceleration interval can feel sharp while remaining continuous. These are definitions and original design reasoning, not reverse-engineered properties of the pack.

The quintic easing function `s(u)=10u³−15u⁴+6u⁵`, for `u` from zero to one, has zero first and second derivatives at its endpoints. It is useful for a genuine arrival or reversal. Applying a fresh zero-velocity ease to *every* waypoint makes a chain of unnecessary mini-stops. Use nonzero endpoint derivatives at transit poses; if adjoining intervals have different durations, match derivatives in seconds rather than copying a normalized slope. A monotone swing-time warp should preserve event order and explicitly chosen boundary derivatives.

**P — evaluated geometry matters:** a continuous bone channel alone does not prove a smooth world-space sole, because hierarchy, rotations and contact correction combine. A rigid shoe can rotate smoothly while one corner crosses the floor. After any timing change, test the composed body/legs/head and the actual export/import path. Loop checks require pose and derivative agreement; finite clips instead require continuous internal junctions and deliberate entry/exit behavior.

**M-local — current review boundary:** [v002](../../../../blender/animation/reviews/player_animation_library_v1/walk_snappy_one_step_v002/README.md) is a finite F1–43/30 FPS four-step study with contacts F1/11.5/22/32.5/43. It starts already walking, retains rigid legs and an acknowledged waist gap, and has no authored Idle-to-Walk entry. Do not wrap the entire 43-sample review as if it were a certified production loop. Its checks establish the reported study constraints; artistic match, runtime starts/stops and mobile integration remain unestablished.

## Small original architecture proposal, after artistic approval

**M-local baseline:** inspected [R6-P runtime](../../../../game_mobile_3d/scripts/player_reference_locomotion_r6p.gd) and [integration report](../../../PRODUCTION_LOCOMOTION_R6P_INTEGRATION.md). They already retain one normalized phase for Walk/Sprint/turn samples and compose weapon behavior through one pose-writing path. The report also explicitly acknowledges foot sliding and desktop-only timing evidence. These are local facts, not Actions & Stuff facts. The pending Snappy studies are not loaded by this path.

**P — smallest useful extension:** keep the current sampler; add data and diagnostics around it before considering an AnimationTree migration.

1. **Clip contract.** Per approved clip, record duration, loop/finite status, left/right receiving/release/pass markers, nominal travel, space convention, track ownership, and reference pose. Avoid assuming equal support duty merely because clips share a normalized period. Finite study markers remain review data until a suitable cycle and starts/stops are authored.
2. **Motion intent and actual displacement.** Read controller speed/direction/grounding without redefining gameplay movement. Preserve the live phase on speed or facing changes. A future distance-driven cadence is an experiment: when blocked against a wall, decide explicitly whether gait stops or continues pushing rather than using requested speed unconditionally.
3. **Support-aware pose evaluation.** Sample approved authored motion first, then apply one bounded correction stage only where evidence shows it is needed. Rigid-leg reach limits must trigger release/replant or a documented visual compromise. Avoid a second skeleton writer fighting the existing weapon path.
4. **Transition policy.** Prefer compatible support and direction; use a short bridge/start/stop when compatibility fails. Keep a captured displayed pose plus useful velocity for interruption, rather than always restarting at a fixed first frame. Waiting for a suitable support may refine visuals, but must have a measured latency bound. No numerical blend duration here is an Oreville value.
5. **Event identity.** Use an unwrapped phase/step counter, side and transition generation to identify accepted receiving events. Detect all markers crossed over an update interval; testing equality against a frame index loses events when rendering skips. Suppress duplicate outgoing/incoming clip notifications. Seeking, pausing and preview scrubbing need an explicit no-gameplay-events policy.
6. **Layer contract and final writer.** Locomotion owns its agreed lower-body channels; carry/aim/action layers own only their agreed channels. Any additive motion needs a known reference pose and delta conversion. Validate the resulting final pose, not just the base clip. Audio/FX receive accepted events from the same dispatcher.

Godot's 4.7 cyclic modes can replace some timing plumbing in a separate evaluated prototype. Cyclic blending requires finite immutable AnimationNodeAnimation lengths; output TimeSeek with unequal lengths can break synchronization. [AnimationTree synchronization restrictions](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/animation/animation_tree.html#sync-mode) That is a reason to compare designs in isolation, not an instruction to rewrite the established sampler or migrate pending clips.

## Mobile and 30 FPS evaluation

**D:** Godot separates fixed physics ticks from rendered frames; default physics frequency is 60 Hz, and interpolation can smooth their mismatch at the cost of displayed delay. [4.7 physics interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/physics/interpolation/physics_interpolation_introduction.html) **P:** retain the tested gameplay tick policy and evaluate the visual/event path at both 30 and 60 rendered FPS. An authored 30 FPS review is not a requirement to run physics at 30 Hz or restrict all curves to integer frames.

**P — sampling arithmetic:** 30 FPS gives 33.33 ms between images. A 55 ms load spans only 1.65 frame intervals, so two native frames can carry most of its visible change. A 45 ms shoulder delay spans 1.35 intervals; a 60 ms head delay spans 1.8. These are conversions of our study timings, not reference measurements. Smooth subframe curves remain valuable, but cannot hide an unreadable native-frame exchange. Check both continuous geometry and actual rendered spacing.

**D:** Godot recommends dedicated position/rotation/scale tracks and notes that cubic interpolation costs more than linear; its Animation reference recommends linear for complex imported 3D animation, accepting a higher sampling rate where needed. [4.7 Animation interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_animation.html) **P:** preserve authored curves in Blender; determine the smallest exported key density that reproduces world contact/rotation within agreed tolerances. Dense authoring validation and dense runtime keys solve different problems.

**D:** profile bottlenecks and compare actual target devices; desktop performance can mislead. Skinning, fill rate, transparency and mobile tile concentration can add costs outside animation scripting. [General optimization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/performance/general_optimization.html), [GPU optimization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/performance/gpu_optimization.html) **P:** start with cached clip/bone access, the current small rig and one final pose composition. Add procedural solves only after a visual need and measured budget exist. Do not infer phone throughput from a 30 FPS encoded video or a desktop headless benchmark.

| Proposed acceptance slice | Capture / measurement | Failure that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| Native gait readability | Fixed front/side/three-quarter at 1x 30 and 60 FPS; every-frame strips plus continuous playback | One-frame recovery teleport, false hold, excess head/waist separation, unwanted double bounce |
| Speed and transition behavior | Start/stop, slow ramp, Walk/Run changes at multiple phases; actual capsule speed and sole anchors | Phase reset, support-side switch, skate, latency hidden by long crossfade |
| Direction / context interruption | 45/90/180° turns, moving landing, repeated input changes, equipped carry/action | Unreachable anchor, leg through floor, torso/weapon conflict, retained stale event |
| Scheduling robustness | Artificial uneven update intervals, wrap, pause/restart, scene re-entry and seeks | Missed/duplicated contact audio, re-fired entry events, phase drift |
| Device performance | Warm and sustained actual phone play; animation CPU time, full frame time, p95/p99 and thermal behavior | Mean FPS conceals hitches; CPU-only timing is presented as total device capacity |

All tests above are proposed; this document runs none of them. No new Run Snappy, production Walk, start/stop clip, contact-audio system or rig change is authorized by these research notes. Continue to the user's artistic feedback before authoring another locomotion revision.
