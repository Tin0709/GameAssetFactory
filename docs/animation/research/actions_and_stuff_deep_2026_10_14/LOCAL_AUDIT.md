## Why numerical passes do not mean an appealing match

Geometry tests answer whether sampled blocks penetrate, detach beyond a chosen bound or lose intended support. They do not answer whether the viewer reads a convincing impulse, pleasing pose, personality, controlled looseness or clear weight transfer. A60.2mm waist gap can pass a75mm guard while still looking disconnected. Zero nonadjacent overlap intentionally excludes adjacent piston seating. Correct foot speed at one nominal straight trajectory says nothing about input interruption, acceleration, rotation or changed terrain.

Continuous velocity/acceleration is useful but not a style score: a perfectly smooth symmetric oscillation can look mechanical; a smooth9.33m/s swing can still concentrate perceptually too much motion into too few30FPS samples. The first v002 spacing draft illustrates why sample distribution matters. More height or higher peak speed does not supply missing pose contrast.

Estimator provenance matters. V027's raw first-order seam0.01253m/s failed the old0.006 bound; second-order0.00377 passed that numerical criterion. V028 similarly retained raw0.01087 versus second-order0.00538. V002 internal maximum second-order0.00345 uses historical0.01-frame spacing; its50-microsecond raw noisy probe is separate. These are different estimators, not proof the old checks passed. None measures human appeal. Finally, normalized2D cap curves and3D head trajectories differ in camera, scale, rotation and landmark meaning; amplitude normalization cannot produce a valid artistic-fit score.
