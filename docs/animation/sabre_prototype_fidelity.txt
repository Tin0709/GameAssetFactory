What is recovered directly
--------------------------
The preferred private MD2_Valorie_Sabre_Source.glb preserves the recovered
32-joint player skeleton, rest transforms, hierarchy, inverse bind matrices,
weights and original animation channel data. Character: 608 vertex entries,
770 triangles. Sabre: 970 vertex entries, 816 triangles, original UVs/normals
and 32px base/MRES maps. Valorie uses the original 64px base/MRES maps.
Original image bytes and mesh attributes/topology are independently compared
to their extracted inputs, including triangle winding.

There are 22 selectable semantic states, backed by 13 distinct original clips:
Idle, Walk, Run, JumpTakeoff, JumpAirborne, JumpLanding, Attack1/2/3,
Recovery1/2/3 and AirAttack. The GLB preserves selected source timestamps,
interpolation and all translation/rotation/scale channels. The 22 aliases
contain 2,112 channels in total; that is not 22 unique recovered animations.

The saved Blender study retains 22 direct-import actions and 22 derived SLERP
precision preview copies. The selected GLB remains authoritative for original
keys and clocks. At five phases for every semantic state, the preview's maximum
joint-world matrix component error was 8.60e-7; grip error was 6.67e-8 source
units. These are sampled validation results, not a claim about Unreal runtime
blending. Saved/reopened rig, selector, timing and expression checks pass16/16.

Original facial-joint tracks remain in these animations. Neutral facial
geometry and pixels are original. The optional Focused/Blink morph overlays
are newly authored controls, not recovered native expression logic.

Fifteen original Sabre Vorbis samples are decoded transiently from Research:
three variants each for Attack1, Attack2, Attack3 swing, Attack3 whoosh and
Sabre heavy-jump swing. Source hashes, Ogg CRCs and durations are verified.

What is reconstructed or inferred
--------------------------------
No native Sabre-to-animation-family row value has been recovered. This study
selects the actual 1H_Medium SwordCombo family; it is an experimental mapping.
The original static sword is attached to J_R_Weapon with an identity item-local
mount. Native equipment/socket offsets have not been recovered.

Nine semantic states reuse original Idle/Walk/Run: StrafeLeft/Right,
CombatReady, ReadyEnter/Exit, CombatWalk/Run and CombatStrafeLeft/Right.
They are labelled adapters, not bespoke recovered ready/strafe/transition clips.
Input buffering, movement speed, gravity, collider, state transitions and blend
durations are prototype logic. Directional movement adaptation and cadence
calibration are described below; they do not recover missing original clips.
Attacks retain full-body clips with reduced travel; there is no recovered
native lower-body blend, gameplay damage system, enemy or hitstop simulation.

Blade trails and finite particle bursts follow actual weapon markers. Their
lifetimes, colours, VFX gates and impact-ready signals are authored. A fast
blade-motion landmark does not establish native contact/notify timing.
The exported components do not recover Unreal's complete particle systems.

The original MRES maps are projected to glTF/Godot metal, roughness and masked
emission. Skin alpha is cutout, separate from the MRES scattering channel.
Original parent shader graphs, scattering scale, material gains, native
reflection, campfire exposure and post-processing are not fully recovered.
The study uses nearest filtering and clamped texture edges as a renderer
projection. The exported source samplers omit wrapS/T (glTF defaults to repeat);
that does not prove the original Unreal sampler. An edge audit found no triangle
centroid colour changes; filtering/wrapping are not certified native settings.
Any mode-local reflection support is a labelled renderer adapter; it does not
modify source material pixels or the existing grass/lighting resources.
The lab's global reflected-light source remains disabled. An optional local
probe supplies sky/environment reflections to the private actor only; use its
Refresh button after changing the lab time of day. This preserves the original
metallic map instead of darkening or painting over its pixels.

Audio sample bytes are original. Attack acceptance schedules a round-robin
variant; its existing decoded amplitude-envelope peak is aligned with the
measured blade-speed landmark. Attack3 schedules swing and whoosh separately.
This fixes the earlier roughly .32s-early finisher whoosh. Alignment uses the
existing 96-bin decode journal (about 5-15ms bin resolution), not a native notify.
The bounded queue drops canceled attacks; mute/reset clears delayed events and
live voices. Variant selection, layering, fixed pitch and -14dB gain are authored.
The native Wwise graph, mixing, offsets and attenuation are not reproduced.
The available heavy-jump sound's assignment to the selected medium air attack
is unproven. Whiffs do not play fictitious enemy impacts.

Movement calibration and remaining physics limits
------------------------------------------------
The source-scale-1 walking/running sole-vertex trajectories support a kinematic
calibration, not a recovered native movement-config value. Contact-velocity
least-squares estimates are 2.00625 source units/s for Walk and 5.63224 for Run.
The source profile targets effective 2.0/5.6 at full-speed clip rate 1.0; the old
lab's 1.2 speed multiplier must not be applied again to this calibration.
Walk contact estimates agree bilaterally around 2.02. Run is asymmetric
(left 4.464, right 6.495); one speed alone cannot remove every stance slip.
These observations do not establish native units in metres or gameplay buffs.
An explicit gameplay-only leg-direction adapter reorients the two source leg
trajectories toward actual travel while leaving hips, spine, arms and weapon
poses unchanged. It preserves each leg's original minimum sole height, including
diagonals; it does not perform native foot locking or erase source contact error.
It restores the source pose before each sample and is bypassed for raw review,
attacks and jumps. The UI exposes a switch for direct comparison.
Locomotion aliases retain normalized stride phase, including Walk/Run changes
with an .08s blend. Grounded braking keeps the gait until stationary; it does not
switch to idle while still sliding. Lateral sprint uses the Run clip and its
direction adapter, rather than playing a Walk alias at running speed.

JumpTakeoff lasts .4s, AirborneLoop .833333s and Landing .3s in the recovered
clips. Root/hip translation in those pose clips cannot establish native height,
distance, gravity or complete airtime. The physical prototype aligns its apex
with the .4s takeoff endpoint and retains an explicitly estimated 1.2-unit height
(ballistic gravity 15, launch velocity 6, nominal airtime .8s). The airborne clip
continues until a real floor contact; then Landing runs. The game's declared
JumpHeight/JumpDistance/JumpCurve/TimeToJumpApex fields do not supply their values.
An in-game measured jump trajectory is still required for exact runtime parity.
The read-only 480-samples-per-cycle audit and threshold sensitivity are retained
in Research/reports/prototype_sabre/source_foot_contact_audit.txt and .json.
