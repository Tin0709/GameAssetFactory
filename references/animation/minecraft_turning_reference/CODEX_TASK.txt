MOTION DESIGN
- Keep recognizable R1 Walk/Sprint rhythm and opposite arm/leg progression.
- Ease into a restrained turn-dependent lean and torso/shoulder bias.
- Fast turning can be more assertive than slow turning, but do not bank
  heavily merely because speed is high on a straight path.
- Maintain small head/torso orientation differences where visually useful;
  no exaggerated anticipation delay, backward head lock, or bob elimination.
- Let arms continue the gait with modest turn balance. Do not freeze them
  or use independent random lag to manufacture complexity.
- A sustained curve should maintain coherent turn intent while stride
  motion continues. No return-to-neutral every footstep/quadrant.
- Return toward the base gait smoothly as curvature decreases.
- Treat left/right relative to actual character/travel orientation, not the
  screen. Mirror as a starting point only; verify both signs and contacts.
- Use small leg/hip corrections only if needed; do not compensate by
  excessive shoulder translations, torso gaps, or foot penetration.
