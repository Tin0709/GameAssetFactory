# Block jump V2 — additive Blender study, review pending

This only proposes **new jump-up and hop-down actions**. All existing idle, walk, sprint, weapon and combat assets remain unchanged. **Latest request (2026-10-09): defer jump animation in the game; use smooth step ascent with the old locomotion.** This study and its separate [pose export](../../../../game_mobile_3d/assets/characters/jump_v2/README.md) are preserved for later and are not active on the player. [V1](../block_jump_v1/README.md) and its `.blend`/videos remain available for comparison. The custom run-in/out in `PREVIEW_ONLY_ContinuousJumpReview_V2` supplies review context and is **not the production locomotion animation**.

Open `block_jump_v2_review.blend`, scene `BLOCK_JUMP_V2_REVIEW`, then play the timeline. `block_jump_gameplay.mp4` and `block_jump_side.mp4` are fixed-camera reviews at real 24 FPS. Frames 1–104 show entry phase A; frames 105–208 show an 8 frame phase offset B with the opposite leading leg. Frame 105 is a labeled preview reset with CONSTANT body/travel keys across the cut, not a proposed gameplay transition. The videos show two different leads rather than identical replay.

Compared with V1, V2 makes three focused changes: reaches a recognizable opposed leg stride within three frames of takeoff, holds that silhouette through flight with only slight phase drift, then quickly resumes gait; keeps the torso almost upright with a small landing recovery; and removes outward arm fan while retaining unequal forward/down arm counterbalance. The held stride is chosen from the next extremum of the entry gait phase, rather than fixing an anatomical lead for every jump. The opposite-phase test verifies this swap. [The side comparison](v1_v2_side_comparison.jpg) shows V1 versus both V2 leads at corresponding phases.

The independent dense reference audit observed entry/switch around source f43–48, held up silhouette f49–61, release f62–65; down entry f110–114, held silhouette f115–125 and a visible lead change by f128. Repeat ascent f360–370 holds the opposite screen-side lead. Those are **visible silhouette windows**, not reconstructed joint angles, exact physical contact times or an anatomical left/right identification. Camera following and occlusion prevent those claims. The original video and diagnostic reference sheets are linked through [V1](../block_jump_v1/README.md); they are not duplicated here.

The 1 m block, 1.5 m/s preview speed, 13 frame ascent, 11 frame fall, three-frame stride entry and 0.025 phase drift are authored fitting assumptions. They are not measurements of the supplied video. Both review cameras, studio materials and lights match V1 for comparison. No moving camera disguises the arc.

The approved full body/rig is copied from the original R14 pass2 source, preserving vertex positions, topology, weights, UVs, packed atlas and 13 rest bones. Full straight arms/hands and the approved 32 mm inward shoulder pose are retained. Root remains unkeyed, ForeArm local rotation remains identity, and no knees, scale animation or new rig bones are introduced. Original source SHA and V1 file SHA are checked; loaded source hold-action keys/handles are also checked unchanged. Byte-identical source protects all old source animations.

`study_manifest.json` records preservation and both leads' evaluated quarter-frame floor/block checks, including the preview reset. The check clips final deformed mesh polygons to the block footprint and checks minimum Z against the block top; all mesh vertices are checked against the ground. Air plateau checks require strongly opposed legs and verify left/right swap from phase +8. These prove nonpenetration and the selected phase scheme; they **do not prove artistic approval, exact foot planting without slip, source physics reconstruction, or future runtime blending**. `fresh_open_audit.json` verifies the saved asset against a fresh append from the original, packed texture dependencies, additive scope and unchanged V1.

Final results: 829 quarter-frame samples, zero floor/block failures, minimum clearance approximately 1 mm, zero ForeArm bend and zero shoulder-inset error. Across 74 plateau samples the opposed leg split stays at least 38.13°; phase +8 swaps the legs with maximum angle error 2.23×10⁻¹⁶ radians. Final trajectory safety correction is below 0.000001 m. Both H.264 videos decode all 208 frames at 960×540, 24 FPS, 8.667 s. Actual encoded samples were visually inspected in both cameras, including the opposite-lead journey, with complete arms/hands and visible takeoff/landing. Review approval remains the user's decision.

Reproduce from this folder using Blender 5.2: `blender -b --factory-startup --python create_study.py`; `blender -b block_jump_v2_review.blend --python verify_fresh_open.py`; `blender -b block_jump_v2_review.blend --python render_study.py`. `package_review.py` makes compact render comparisons; `check_encoded.py` decodes the actual videos with cv2/Pillow. Intermediate frames, logs and dense validation remain ignored under `.validation/`. Review status remains pending until the user explicitly approves the new jump motion.
