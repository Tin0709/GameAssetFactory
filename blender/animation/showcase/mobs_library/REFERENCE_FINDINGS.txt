Timing and curves
Adult movement is a 0.75-second loop. The numeric leg rotation and translation
tracks use uneven key spacing with matching first/last values. The two legs are
offset by half a cycle. Uneven spacing shapes the exchange and recovery; this
is more expressive than uniform sinusoidal leg swinging. Matching endpoint
values alone does not establish velocity continuity or foot planting.

Body vertical motion and head pitch use twice the stride frequency; lateral
head motion, waist twist/roll and arm motion use the stride frequency. Phase
offsets separate the peaks. The alternate adult move retains the same leg
tracks but changes upper-body behavior. This shows how the same stepping
mechanism can read differently with another upper-body layer.

Idle is not one fixed exported clip: multiple posture variants contain
life-time-driven Molang oscillations plus fixed asymmetric offsets. Different
left/right arm baselines, arm phase offsets, head cant, waist rotation and
opposed leg postures give the zombie an uneven silhouette. The oscillation
frequencies vary between variants. The prototype should select its own finite
loop duration and explicitly author a smooth closing boundary.

Keyframes versus runtime behavior
Numeric timestamp dictionaries are keyframed data. Molang strings require
runtime evaluation. Adult upper-body motion is largely expression-driven;
idle variants are mostly procedural. A smoothed on-ground variable gates some
vertical/head/arm motion. The explicit head-look layer is relative to entity
rotation and depends on target queries. These behaviors do not automatically
become Blender curves through a JSON import.

Controllers apply movement-speed weights, choose posture variants, switch
between target/no-target and water states, and use transition blends around
0.13–0.30 seconds in the inspected relevant states. Speed weighting must not
be confused with a cadence/time-update expression. The adult move itself has
no explicit anim_time_update property. The target-pose clip does have a
runtime-dependent clock and Catmull-Rom keys, with the arms and head entering
at different times. Its held endpoint is a pose layer, not a locomotion loop.

The alias zombie_pose resolves to a riding pose, not the normal idle posture.
Do not infer animation purpose from a generic alias without tracing its caller.

Skeleton implications
The default zombie has root -> waist -> body -> head, with arms parented to
body. Legs use separate root-level helper parents, so body bounce is not
necessarily inherited by them. Limbs are rigid blocks without elbow/knee
chains. Additional item/facial helpers are outside this prototype's scope.
Our Blender rig has legs parented to Hips; reproducing a support principle
therefore requires original local compensation, not copied reference curves.

Original prototype direction (proposed, not authored)
Idle: 4-second/120-frame loop at 30 FPS, restrained uneven weight transfer,
slight hunch, unequal forward arm droop and delayed head response.
Walk: 1.4-second/42-frame loop at 30 FPS, low rigid-leg shamble, unequal step
accents, visible grounded support and delayed chest/head response.
Use new poses, timings and periodic curves; fixed limb scales, stationary Root.
Measure evaluated sole corners and compensate local hip/leg transforms offline.
Document nominal travel speed; in-place contact must be evaluated with the
corresponding presentation travel. Playback excludes duplicate closing keys.
No copied reference numbers/curves will be used to generate either Action.
