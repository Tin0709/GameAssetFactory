RECOVERY LEG STRATEGY AND RESULT
V2 uses shaped whole-leg pitch arcs, slight root paths, pelvis rotation and nonuniform rise/fall. Walk authored extrema are approximately +24 / -59 degrees; Sprint +35 / -77, with smooth interpolation. No shortening, scaling, new bones or deformation.
Passing retains a small root lift. At deepest rear recovery, the root routes down/back rather than pushing the thigh through the torso: up to about 20 mm down for Walk / 55 mm for Sprint and 10 mm back. This is a deliberate rigid-joint compromise, not anatomical knee motion. Early lifted peaks caused torso contact; the corrected root path removes the diagnostic intersections without flattening the recovery arc.
Front consecutive frames show stronger support/recovery length contrast than V1, especially Walk f9 (0.333 s) and Sprint f1 (0.000 s). Opposite-side roles recur half a cycle later. Compactness is view-dependent: rear/elevated views and the side diagnostic still reveal the long straight segment. This does not fully reproduce the source silhouette.

ARMS
Forward arms now combine pitch, axial twist, inward presentation and a small shoulder path. Approximate peak forward pitch is 56 degrees Walk / 79 Sprint; rear swings remain controlled at roughly -32 / -42. Forward axial twist is about 16–18 degrees Walk and 18–19 Sprint, weighted toward the forward peak rather than applied to the whole swing. Small chosen left/right pitch and twist differences avoid mirror-perfect arms; no random noise.
Sprint's shoulder shifts outward by up to 40 mm and forward 24 mm near its peak to clear the head/chest. Rear arms do not inherit this peak displacement. This produces stronger front presentation but still a blockier, less tightly gathered silhouette than the source, particularly from the rear. No elbow or hand was added.

HIPS, TORSO AND SHOULDERS
Hips use shaped weight-transfer and clearance profiles instead of a pure sinusoid. Lateral excursion is about +/-14 mm Walk / +/-18 mm Sprint, with gait-specific yaw and roll. Root remains identity, without accumulated world travel.
Walk's phase samples show Contact hips Z 0.674 m, Down 0.665, Passing 0.705 and Up 0.710, repeated on the other side. These are authored phase labels, not claimed source contacts. Walk retains an upright, smaller pose vocabulary rather than slowed Sprint.
Sprint has stronger forward trunk posture, hip/shoulder counter-motion and larger reach/recovery contrast; it reads differently in a still pose. Its pelvis rises soon after the reach/contact pose (Z 0.636 at phase 0, 0.659 at .10, 0.728 at .25). A separate downward acceptance after reach is less evident than in Walk and remains an artistic review item.
Spine and Chest counter-yaw peak at different phases (about .022 and .055 cycle offsets), with separately shaped pitch/roll response. The changes are selective, not a global rotation multiplier or universal delay. Total hips bob rises from 4.39 to 5.23 cm Walk and 9.70 to 11.01 cm Sprint.

HEAD / NECK
Restrained rotational compensation retains about 24% of the Chest orientation delta at the Head. Head orientation remains near rest: about 0.95–1.65 degrees Walk and 2.15–3.21 Sprint. It inherits positional movement: head vertical excursion is 5.37 cm Walk and 11.22 cm Sprint (V1: 4.47 / 10.06). No world lock or vertical bob cancellation was introduced.

GROUND, LOOP AND TECHNICAL EVIDENCE
Sampled at 1/16-frame increments including between authored keys: 257 Walk and 209 Sprint samples per version. Minimum leg bottom stays about 4 mm above ground in V2. Maximum simultaneous clearance is 58.4 mm Walk / 95.8 mm Sprint, versus 40 / 66 mm in V1. Brief airborne poses are visible. Human playback should judge whether the Walk rise feels buoyant or too floaty; perfect foot locking is neither claimed nor expected.
No major inset cuboid intersections in the corrected samples. This diagnostic uses a 15 mm inset and excludes the upper 70 mm of the leg's intentional hip seam; it does not claim zero surface/joint overlap.
Loop endpoint curve error: zero. Maximum terminal slope error: approximately 1.49e-6. Root matrix error: zero. No scale or WeaponCarrier curves. Rigid cuboid corner-distance error below 1.3e-7 m in V2. Limb lengths and geometry remain unchanged.
Preview Action slots are bound correctly; sampled evaluated preview channels exactly match their curves. See validation.json and preview_validation.json. Encoded video frame counts, 24 FPS timing, dimensions and decode checks are recorded in video_validation.json.

REMAINING MISMATCH / RIG LIMIT
Specific evidence: front Walk source f25–30 (0.833–1.000 s), candidate V2 f9–13 (0.333–0.500 s); front Sprint source f90–95 (3.000–3.167 s), candidate f1–5 (0.000–0.167 s). Source and candidate are only approximately aligned. The reference recovery/arm grouping still appears more compact in these sequences. Rear Sprint source f205–210 (6.833–7.000 s) versus candidate f1–5 also shows remaining blocky arm/leg presentation.
Sprint V2 side f1 / 0.000 s (sprint_v2_side/000.png) demonstrates the hard geometric limit: the whole 0.675 m leg can foreshorten in front projection, but it cannot independently fold its lower half behind the upper half. A knee joint with segmented geometry could provide that independent fold; an elbow could independently wrap the lower arm. The source topology is unknown, so these are possible articulations, not claims about the reference rig. This first pass does not justify that complexity before human evaluation at gameplay size. No rig change was made or requested.
At actual gameplay scale the stronger Sprint reach and raised forward arm are readable, but fine cross-body and torso differences are subtle. A livelier overall feel cannot be approved from sampled stills alone.

TURNING COMPATIBILITY
Turning V1 should be rebased after straight V2 is approved. Significant changes: Leg.L/R pitch timing and location paths; Arm.L/R pitch/twist/inward motion and shoulder locations; Hips vertical/lateral paths and rotations; Spine/Chest phase and posture; corresponding Neck/Head compensation. Root, cadence, hierarchy and normalized cycle convention remain compatible.
Clean adaptation is feasible by applying/refining the prior bounded turning offsets on the new V2 bases at shared phase. Existing turning Actions store complete V1-based poses, so reusing them unchanged in a blend would pull the character toward the old base. Future work should recheck ground, hip/shoulder clearance, head behavior and turn entry/exit. No turning adaptation was performed.
