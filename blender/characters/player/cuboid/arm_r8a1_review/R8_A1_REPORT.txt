1. RIG CHANGES

File: ../player_longgun_arm_rig_v2_study.blend
Author rig: R8A1_RigV2_Author / R8A1_RigV2_Skeleton.
The copied Arm.L/R bones became UpperArm.L/R, each 337.5 mm long.
Connected ForeArm.L/R bones add another 337.5 mm per side. Total bone count
is 13, up from 11; total arm length remains 675 mm per side. All other bone
rest transforms, hierarchy and lengths are unchanged.

Each original arm cuboid is now two independently rigid cuboids of the same
225 mm width/depth. All vertices have exactly one bone weight of 1.0.
Faces remain flat; no smoothing, rounding, organic elbow deformation, fingers,
face rig, hand bones, stretch, or arm translations were introduced.
The hand is the terminal part of each forearm, as requested for minimalism.
Arm-side UVs follow the existing atlas with interpolation at the elbow cut;
the new elbow caps use existing end-face UVs. Non-arm geometry and UVs match
the source exactly. Legs and locomotion were not changed.

There are no new bone constraints or persistent helper controls. Two-link
geometry was used offline to choose the shoulder/elbow/hand pose, then baked
as ordinary FK quaternion rotations. This is not a runtime IK rig. The
existing WeaponCarrier mount relationship is retained in the study copies.

New Actions:

- LongGunAimBias_RigV2_Study_V1

Validation preserves 9,599 existing tracked files, all 25 source Actions,
all source mesh geometry and rig signatures. No production or Godot edits.
Original and R7-W1 assets remain intact. See preservation.json and validation.json.

2. HOLD STUDY RESULT

Stock-to-shoulder: the buttstock now overlaps the right shoulder/upper-arm
pocket and reads as received by the body. Its center is approximately
(-0.431, -0.089, 1.310) m. This is a stylized seated contact, with measurable
interpenetration noted below; it is not claimed to be a perfect surface fit.

Trigger arm: the elbow sits lower and behind the grip, while the forearm
returns upward to the receiver. The elbow bends approximately 81.6 degrees
from straight. This compact return was impossible with the previous rigid
beam. The grip no longer requires the R7-W1 shoulder translation.

Support arm: the elbow has a different, more forward shape and bends about
41.1 degrees. The hand supports the fore-end roughly 280 mm forward along
the gun's axis from the grip (weapon scale is the unchanged 1.12). Its center
is lower than the fore-end body, avoiding the first candidate's deeper
through-gun placement. Both hand centers meet their selected geometric
targets to floating-point precision. These are authored contact targets,
not evidence of anatomically realistic fingers or palms.

Rifle/body relationship: grip position is (-0.180,-0.340,1.160) m: about
116 mm closer along forward depth and 191 mm higher than original A. The
rifle points diagonally across the upper body. Changing its direction as
well as adding elbows is what allows distinct fore-end support without
longer arms. Relative to previous B, the grip is 100 mm closer along depth
and 20 mm higher. These values are coordinate comparisons, not clearance.

Torso/shoulders: restrained spine/chest yaw and forward participation help
receive the stock; shoulders keep their normal joint positions. The upper
arms can descend independently of the upward-pointing forearms, producing
the compact structure missing in B.

Head/gaze: the base head turns toward the diagonal gun line and inclines
slightly. The eyes remain visible in the principal front/gameplay views.
The result suggests weapon focus; exact eye-through-aperture alignment is
not established by this study or by the supplied perspective stills.

3. AIM-BIAS RESULT

The new aim Action starts from the RigV2 hold, focuses, adds a small emphasis,
and settles. Frames 1–48 play at 24 fps; frame 49 matches the first pose.
Markers: HOLD 1, FOCUS 15, EMPHASIS 20, SETTLED 43.
Peak local head offset is 1.2/1.0/-1.1 degrees, with small neck participation
and 0.25 degrees of chest participation. Combined world head change peaks
at 2.67 degrees. No full recoil, muzzle effect, or firing system was created.

The ordered close-view frames show a small focus gesture rather than a broad
tilt. At gameplay distance it is barely distinguishable from C. This is
secondary life, not a strong gameplay signal. Two decoded 48-frame videos
provide shared-scene and close-up playback; final timing/artistic judgement
is left to the human reviewer.

5. LIMITATIONS

The minimal articulation meaningfully resolves the main rigid-arm blocker.
No further skeletal complexity is demonstrated as necessary for this hold.
This does not certify this rig for every future reload, holster, or extreme
arm pose, and existing one-piece-arm Actions cannot simply be applied to the
renamed/split arm bones without an explicit future migration/retarget pass.

There are visible hard elbow seams and intersecting cuboid corners. These
are the rigid-segment construction, not soft skin deformation. The forearms
and hands remain chunky; wrist orientation cannot be controlled separately.
Hand bones were not needed to demonstrate this hold, but that choice may
be reconsidered only if a later approved pose requires independent wrists.

Contact checks use weapon triangles against boxes inset 5 mm from the body
surfaces. C and sampled D frames 1,7,15,20,23,31,43,49 have zero weapon/head,
weapon/chest, or weapon/left-upper-arm intersections. This is a bounded
sampled test, not exhaustive all-pairs collision validation.

The stock intersects the right upper-arm/shoulder box (35 weapon triangles;
maximum sampled weapon-vertex depth about 61 mm). The trigger forearm/hand
has 66 intersecting triangles, about 68 mm maximum vertex depth. The support
forearm/hand has 107, about 63 mm. These topology-dependent counts/depths
are proxies and do not mean that every visible contact has that penetration.
They do mean contact polish is still needed: do not describe these as all
minor or fully clean. Some insertion into fingerless block hands and a seated
stock is visually useful, but excess receiver/wrist or stock/sleeve burial
should be reduced during hold polish before any production replacement.

Quarter-frame sampling verifies unchanged root/hips/legs, no arm translation,
no scale animation, and exact loop endpoint agreement. All mesh weights are
rigid and the non-arm surface/UV data is preserved. These checks protect the
asset; they do not replace human visual approval.

6. RECOMMENDATION

RIGV2 IS GOOD ENOUGH; NEXT STEP SHOULD BE PRODUCTION HOLD/HOLSTER UPGRADE.

This recommendation concerns the minimal arm structure: the two-segment FK
arms solve the compact elbow/forearm problem without more bones or controls.
After human review, the next separate task should polish the contacts and
author/migrate hold and holster for the chosen rig, with compatibility checks.
The current study pose is not a drop-in production-approved animation.
No production replacement, holster rewrite, gameplay integration, reload,
or recoil system is included in this pass.
